# PCA_and_DWT
This project explores image fusion approaches for remote sensing applications, focusing on the integration of Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) and optical (RGB) imagery. Two independent fusion models are implemented: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT). In the PCA-based approach, the RGB image is transformed into principal components, and the intensity information is enhanced using SAR data to preserve spectral integrity while adding structural detail. In the DWT-based approach, both SAR and RGB images are decomposed into frequency sub-bands, where low-frequency components maintain overall intensity and high-frequency components preserve edges and textures, followed by inverse transformation to reconstruct the fused image. 
Image fusion in remote sensing offers significant advantages, including the ability to combine complementary information from multiple sensors, enhance spatial and spectral resolution, improve interpretability, increase robustness under adverse environmental conditions, and support a wide range of applications such as classification, change detection, vegetation monitoring, and disaster assessment.
